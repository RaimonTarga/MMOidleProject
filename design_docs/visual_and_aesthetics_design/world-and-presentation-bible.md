# World & Presentation Bible v0

**Purpose:** Baseline setting, tone, and presentation reference for the game.  
This document defines the coherent world vision that future sprite, UI, SFX, VFX, menu, monster, item, and lore decisions should obey.

This is not an art style guide yet.  
This is not a full lore bible yet.  
This is the conceptual foundation.

---

## 1. Core Premise

The game takes place in a **shattered ascension-realm**: a dreamlike lattice of broken domains where unbound souls cultivate themselves through combat, essence, gear, and repeated trials.

The world does not obey normal geography. It obeys affinity, memory, pressure, and spiritual law. A swamp can sit beside a mountain, and a volcano can border a tundra, because each zone is not merely a place — it is a fragment of a rule.

Players are faceless, genderless spirits. They begin as unformed vessels and gain definition through the paths they cultivate, the gear they forge, the domains they master, and the trials they survive.

There is no kingdom to save, no chosen hero, and no central villain. The purpose of the journey is ascension: to become stronger, stranger, more defined, and eventually godlike.

---

## 2. Core Fantasy

The player fantasy is **ascension through repetition**.

Players start as fragile spirits and gradually become powerful beings through automated combat, essence harvesting, biome mastery, crafting, class evolution, boss trials, and cooperation with other players.

The fantasy is inspired by immortal cultivation and ARPG power progression, but expressed through an idle MMO structure. Power should feel earned through endurance, refinement, and mastery rather than twitch execution.

The player is not performing heroic quests.

The player is cultivating a vessel.

---

## 3. Player Characters

Player characters are **unbound souls**.

They have no fixed face, gender, origin, species, or human identity. Their lack of defining traits is intentional: they are vessels that players project into.

Visual identity should come from:

- silhouette,
- armor shape,
- weapon shape,
- aura,
- class effects,
- tier progression,
- biome materials,
- and cultivated path.

Faces should generally remain hidden by hoods, helmets, masks, shadow, light, or abstraction.

The character is not male or female. The character is a spirit taking form.

---

## 4. The World

The world is a shattered dream-realm built from domains.

Each node is a fragment. Each biome is a domain. Each domain expresses a law: endurance, speed, force, decay, depth, heat, death, silence, pressure, hunger, recursion, or another primal principle.

The map is not a realistic continent. It is a spiritual topology.

Zones are connected because their laws touch, not because their climates make sense.

---

## 5. Biomes

A biome is not just an environment. It is a rule expressed three ways:

1. enemy behavior,
2. equipment mechanics,
3. visual and audio identity.

For example, a fast forest biome is not merely a forest. It is the law of speed, pursuit, evasion, and living motion.

A mountain biome is not merely a mountain. It is the law of weight, impact, endurance, and thresholds.

Biome identity should remain readable. The player should be able to infer the biome's threat from its enemies, visuals, and item names.

---

## 6. Monsters

Monsters are not random fantasy creatures. They are dream-forms, domain-beasts, failed vessels, constructs, residues, or spirits shaped by the laws of their biome.

However, basic monster names should remain readable and familiar.

The game should prefer clear creature names such as:

- wolf,
- boar,
- bear,
- toad,
- beetle,
- serpent,
- ram,
- golem,
- salamander,
- drake,
- viper,
- stag,
- moth,
- crawler,
- colossus.

The mystical layer should appear through modifiers, descriptions, boss names, item text, and late-game enemies — not by making every early monster abstract.

Recommended naming pattern:

- Early common enemy: readable creature name with light biome flavor.
- Elite enemy: stronger biome modifier or stranger creature type.
- Boss: mythic title, domain title, or named guardian.
- Late-game anomaly: more abstract, system-like, or metaphysical name.

Example escalation:

- Wolf
- Flicker Wolf
- Elder Flicker Wolf
- Gnarled Greatbear
- The Green Hunger

The player should understand the enemy's approximate threat before reading any lore.

---

## 7. Bosses

Bosses are concentrated domain trials.

A boss is not just a larger monster. It is the biome's law made unavoidable. It tests whether the player has understood the biome's damage shape and prepared a fitting answer.

Bosses should feel like gatekeepers, old beasts, domain guardians, failed ascendants, or powerful echoes.

They should not feel like action-game spectacle bosses that require manual dodging.

The boss is the exam.  
The biome is the lesson.

---

## 8. Progression Meaning

Tier progression represents a soul becoming more defined and more capable of surviving higher laws.

A higher tier is not simply a harder area. It is a deeper or higher layer of the ascension-realm.

Progression can be framed as:

- gathering essence,
- refining the vessel,
- mastering domains,
- surviving thresholds,
- passing guardians,
- and ascending into more complex laws.

New tiers should feel like both a power gain and a conceptual widening of the world.

---

## 9. Cooperation

The world does not ask players to save it. It invites them to ascend together.

Cooperation is not a military alliance against evil. It is shared cultivation. Players gather in the same domain, endure the same pressure, harvest the same essence, and grow faster together.

Parties should feel like small groups of spirits training, refining, and challenging thresholds together.

Solo play remains valid. Party play is a generous acceleration, not a requirement except for optional high-end challenges.

---

## 10. Simulation Layer

The world may eventually reveal signs that its spiritual laws are also system laws.

This should be subtle early and stranger later.

Early game:

- dreamlike cultivation realm,
- primal domains,
- readable beasts,
- soft mystery.

Mid game:

- repeated patterns,
- impossible ecology,
- artificial boundaries,
- rule-behavior becoming visible.

Late game:

- glitches,
- custodians,
- arbiters,
- null entities,
- broken protocols,
- recursive beings,
- system-like gods.

Avoid overly modern or comedic terms too early. Words like `admin`, `bug`, `server`, and `glitch` should be used carefully, if at all.

The simulation layer should not replace the spiritual layer. It should deepen it.

Core idea:

> To ascend is to gain authority over reality.

---

## 11. Tone

The tone should be:

- dreamlike,
- primal,
- spiritual,
- mysterious,
- lightly surreal,
- progression-focused,
- cooperative,
- and readable.

The world should not be too grimdark, too comedic, too human-political, or too generic-fantasy.

It should feel strange, but not random.

It should feel soft-lore, not exposition-heavy.

It should feel like a place with rules, even when those rules are not fully explained.

---

## 12. What This Game Is Not

This is not a save-the-world story.

This is not a kingdom fantasy.

This is not a hero-versus-demon-lord plot.

This is not a conventional village/NPC questing MMO.

This is not a game about chosen identities, human factions, or fixed protagonists.

This is not a purely abstract dreamscape where nothing is readable.

This is a game about faceless souls cultivating themselves through the broken laws of a shattered dream.

---

## 13. Working Thesis

> Faceless souls cultivate themselves through the broken laws of a shattered dream, gathering essence, forming vessels, and ascending together toward godhood.
