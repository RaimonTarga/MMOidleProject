# T3 Tundra class/frame 01 — Astra preparation brief

**Next assignment: Astra prepares; Luna executes and publishes.** This replaces the completed Desert/Guard briefs as the next experiment. It is a design/preparation instruction, not an executable packet or a claim that combat has been launched.

**Scope:** 18 T3 identities × 2 seeds = **36 primary observations**, plus 9 deliberately selected alternative packages × 2 seeds = **18 additional observations**. **54 fresh observations maximum.** One ordinary Tundra fixture; ten-minute caps. No bosses, new numerical balance changes, T2 extension, or crossed gear/ability search.

## 1. Decision, intended play, and boundaries

Determine how the six roots and their three frames handle T3 sparse elite/control pressure with sensible, legal preparation. Test a few weapon or ability alternatives where a reference choice could otherwise distort a frame assessment. Preserve meaningful asymmetry; neither identical clear times nor universal ten-minute survival is required.

The designer's Tundra strategy: durable preparation, finish elite engagements before Chill accumulates, defend burst where appropriate, and use slowing/rooting plus kiting on ranged builds. This is an intended strategy family, not a requirement that everyone uses identical armor or one Guard. The designer's new equipment suggestions are hypotheses and permitted starting points, not measured rankings.

**Ask before inventing:** use the provided intent and suggestions without asking the designer to repeat them. Resolve ordinary catalogue details and legal selections during preparation. When a material unexplained failure, unsupported mechanic, or design conflict would require another strategy or gameplay change, return the specific question before creating that response. An ordinary gameplay death does not stop the rest of the approved queue. Do not add a rescue arm, alter a failed build mid-run, or keep testing until it passes.

The aim is a class/frame assessment using existing evidence plus this contrast—not a requirement to perfect all items before judging classes. Unresolved hypotheses and legitimate weak matchups are acceptable outcomes.

## 3. Availability and nomenclature established from the catalogue

- The T3 Volcanic fast weapon is **Cinderlash**, `volcanic-cinderlash`, authored at 1.65 attacks/s with flurry. This resolves the designer's informal "Volcano rapier" wording; do not substitute the Jungle on-hit rapier.
- **Cataclysm Axe**, `cave-cataclysm-axe`, is the medium-speed Cave weapon (1.20 attacks/s, authored every-fifth dead swing). It is not a slow heavy maul simply because it is called an axe.
- **Avalanche Maul**, `mountain-avalanche-maul`, is the T3 Mountain empowered-hit weapon; **Permafrost Maul**, `tundra-permafrost-maul`, is the separate brittle/plating-reduction weapon. Tundra's **Rimebrand** is a different frost-DoT weapon, not that maul.
- **Frenzy, Quick Strike, Detonate, Binding Strike, and Break Free** are available at T3. **Endure and Hamstring** are already available earlier. Use native player-tier rank resolution.
- **Execute Stance** is T3. **Time to Strike, Reaper, and Recuperating Stance** are T4 and must not enter these T3 preparations. Do not confuse Recuperating Stance with Recuperate, the T4 Guard.
- Core examples available by T3 include Tempered/Survivalist/Force from T2 and Accelerant, Arcanist, Bruiser, Duelist, and Sniper at T3. Enumerate actual eligible options and gates locally; this is not a claim that the example list is exhaustive. No T4 cores or relics.
- Accelerant authors attack speed with an outgoing-damage penalty; Arcanist authors Technique power/cooldown benefits, not general Guard cooldown reduction. Inspect the class/adapters before declaring either best. Tempered is the approved fallback when no clear mechanism favors another core.

These are authored catalogue facts, not DPS/EHP rankings. Compare complete real items: the Cinderlash/Cave swap changes Attack, cadence, and item mechanics together, not "attack speed only."

## 5. Automation, posture, boots, and defensive allocation

**No generic Flee/low-health retreat rule.** The old catalogue shows `inside-telegraph -> step-back`, not the generic `flee` action. Keep deliberate telegraph dodging where relevant to the designer's strategy; do not remove it by mislabelling it as fleeing. Show exact rule IDs and costs. If the actual selected receipt includes a generic Flee rule, remove it before freezing the new baseline, with the difference declared.

Retain Find Enemies, Recover First, and relevant Avoid Hazards. Ranged builds use deliberate Keep Distance/Orbit rather than inheriting or deleting movement behavior accidentally. Do not force Orbit on a package whose approved class behavior is explicitly stationary. No new autonomous escape strategy or private controller.

Hamstring is the default T3 ranged control tool. Binding Strike is the one explicit alternative below. Use native ability delivery; include control in the legal budget, displacing an optional offense choice where necessary. Do not append it for free to a 36/38-RP template. Do not use the T4 low-HP Rune in these T3 rows.

Second Wind, Cleanse, and an appropriate burst/sustained Guard are the defensive starting tools. **Brace is the starting burst Guard; Endure is allowed for an explicitly justified tanking package, not automatically added to every failure or banned merely because it is powerful.** Report which results depend on Endure. Retain current values; there is no Endure nerf or ablation matrix. Do not make both Brace and Endure mandatory for all 18 identities.

Posture is chosen during preparation from the approved durable-melee / controlled-kiting strategy. Defensive is a credible tanking option; Offensive remains available where the proposed kiting/pressure package uses it. Do not inherit Offensive blindly, normalize all stances to one choice, or change stance inside a pair. No dynamic Execute experiment here.

Boots: Mountain is the melee approach starting point; Desert is the ranged-kiting starting point. Swamp boots are permitted for Tundra control resistance only after reading which components of Chill/slow the modifier actually affects. Do not promise immunity to Chill, roots, or other effects. If the resistance does not cover the intended component, use the already-authorized Mountain/Desert alternative and state why; do not open a mechanics-repair campaign. Cave boots remain an approved anti-overpull option where a concrete package needs it, not an extra pair.

Charms: use Swamp/Jungle as credible sparse/long-fight options and Mountain for the specified Spirit package. Volcanic's successful swarm evidence remains valid but does not make it the default for every elite fight. Record exact charm choice and its tradeoff; no new charm factorial.

**Tundra armor:** the designer reports overly lenient moving-stack decay. This was not independently reproduced by the command-center catalogue read. Exclude Glacial Bulwark from this packet's primary and paired builds so class conclusions do not depend on that known/questioned strength. Retain it as a separate armor-behavior/tuning backlog item. Do not fix it, exploit it intentionally, or change its decay mid-run. This exclusion is not a claim that every historical Tundra-armor result is invalid.
