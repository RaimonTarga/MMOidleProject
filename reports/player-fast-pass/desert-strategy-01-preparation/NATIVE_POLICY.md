## Targeting

`shared/src/runeDatabase.ts` evaluates rules in order; the first active rule claims each channel independently. The added `in-combat -> focus-lowest-hp` rule is first and claims TARGETING only. It costs 1+2 RP, is T4, and sets native `priorityMode=lowest-hp`. All earlier movement/Guard/recovery rules keep their relative order. Qualification checks native recipe unlocks, fragment ownership, loadout legality and actual RP. This uses the established synthetic mature-package acquisition path, not free behavior or evidence of live acquisition.

`server/src/systems/combat/ai/targetPriority.ts` scores **current absolute HP**, normalized against the maximum current HP in the eligible set: `1 - hp/hpScale`, with a small distance penalty (0.001 weight). It is not HP fraction, max HP, monster role, or an unconditional dealer selector. The selector considers living monsters in the same node, rejects invulnerable/concealed or disallowed bosses, deferred approaches and inaccessible candidates, and applies native acquisition/leash gates. If any eligible monsters are aggroed on the owner, HP preference is scoped to that engaged subset. Rooted owners can select only monsters already in attack reach. Reachability and hazard-aware paths remain production behavior. A valid current target persists unless the best score exceeds its score by the native 25% switch margin. A wounded controller can therefore remain preferred to a dealer.

**Champion constraint discovered and fixed at the user's request:** the prior `summoner/ai.ts` chose nearest-to-owner regardless of the owner's Rune. The user first accepted an indirect-positioning test, then explicitly superseded that decision by requesting a canonical production fix. The new code consumes the existing winning TARGETING channel and the latest `getAutoTargetId(owner)` selection. It does not implement another comparator. Active rules override ordinary nearest and swarm-spread policies; an invalid, concealed, invulnerable, other-node or out-of-leash selection yields no inherited target, rather than a silent nearest fallback. No active rule (or owner auto disabled) retains the original formation policy. Explicit focus and move commands retain priority. Actual attacks still require the summon to reach its target; movement remains leash-clamped.

World mechanics drive summons before the owner's auto-selection later in the tick; inheritance therefore consumes the most recently completed owner decision, normally on the next tick. Tests verify real World tick convergence and native low/high HP channel arbitration, commands, stale death, leash and inactive-rule fallback. `formationTarget.ts` continues to report actual minion targets. No explicit focus command is injected by this experiment. `DESIGNER_DECISION.json` is the scope-change receipt. All arms now share the new production source; historical Champion results predate this fix. The new comparison does not separately measure old versus fixed code efficacy.

## Bond roles and control

The T4 source definitions in `shared/src/data/monsters/desert.monsters.ts` pair each alpha controller with one `sandspitter-cobra` (display name Sunshield Scarab). Source HP values: Sand Viper 4,029; Dune Basilisk 9,006; Dune Tyrant 6,952; Scarab 569. The Scarab is a ranged kiter with a regenerating shield. These are authored definitions, not a claim about every generated encounter. Actual fixture HP/position readbacks and seeded initial roster hashes are retained. Runtime `inPack.packId` supplies membership; temporal adjacency never defines a pair.

Both arms retain Second Wind, Brace, Cleanse in that order. The actual Guard ranks/effects/triggers/cooldowns are in `APPLIED_ARMS.json`. Cleanse uses its native `has-debuff` trigger and deterministic deepest-stack-first removal, breaking ties by effect ID; it skips instanced and immune effects. Desert charged roots are represented as `slow` with `speedMult=0` in `combat/engine/combat.ts`, and `slow` is harmful and cleanseable under `shared/src/systems/monsterDebuffs.ts` / `statusPolicy.ts`. Consequently existing Cleanse can remove this root representation. This is not a change to Cleanse and does not guarantee activation, removal before death, or useful movement afterward. The recorder retains actual activations, pre/post effects, rooted state and position to assess that separately.

## Measurement contract

`strategy-events.jsonl` records 100ms pre/post owner and formation targets, manual-command state, individual minion targets, control, positions, and monsters with actual pack IDs, roles, HP, aggro and visibility. Role labels are observation-only. `summary.strategy.bonds` uses authoritative kill events to label dealer-first, controller-first, same-tick or neither-killed; reports clear/unfinished/ambiguous status and first simultaneous engagement. Missing bodies without a kill and changing membership are ambiguous. Additional members are not silently paired. No simultaneous/AoE ordering is inferred inside a tick. Non-bonded monsters remain in the raw state stream.

“Both engaged” means both roles have sampled attack/aggro on the owner or its minions. Pressure runs from first simultaneous engagement until the dealer's kill and includes temporary disengagement; it reports all incoming owner HP damage and absorption, not damage attributed exclusively to that pair. Intervals may overlap and must not be summed across pairs. Control exposure is sampled root/slow time. No both-engaged exposure is uninformative for strategy, even if monsters die. An unfinished-at-player-death record is not a killed pair.

Exact selector eligibility including native path search is not exposed by the existing recorder. `eligibleDealer.value=null` carries that reason; dealer presence, aggro, visibility, owner root and actual selections are recorded. Do not relabel presence as proven eligibility. Formation targets alone are not damage delivery; use authoritative outgoing damage and Conduit histories. Replacement payments are separate from owner incoming damage. Guard pipeline damage is not HP damage. Source-specific effective healing and overheal remain unavailable.

Use lifetime `completedKills` separately from endpoint `work.kills`. Interval work uses `work.kills` consistently. Missing post-death endpoints are null in the report; the dispatcher keeps an absent endpoint entry and null interval count. A ten-minute cap is not thirty-minute endurance; two seeds are not a population win probability or economy proof. Tick event times use existing tick-start labels; endpoint states follow completed 100ms steps.
