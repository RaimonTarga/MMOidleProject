Combat readout — mockup (wave 0)

One grammar for the numbers, the crit, the overhead bars, a boss frame with quarters, the target frame's chrome and the vitals. Every scene below is driven by real event shapes (player-hit, dot-tick, monster-hit with absorbed, monster-cast-start/-end) at real T1 numbers (Moss Rat 160 HP, Crag Behemoth 2100 HP and its 2.4 s Crag Charge for 95, a T1 pool of 220–240), fed through a port of the code the build will ship: band selection by share, DoT coalescing per second, the per-target lane stack, the bar chip, the quarter markers. Bodies and portraits are the game's own atlas frames (client/public/assets/sprites.png); open this file from the repo so the relative path resolves. Structure and tokens only — no ornament (design §26.4).

Number language for the scenes: · Replay loops; reduced-motion is honoured.

The two number languages, side by side

The same event streams, twice. A sets a floater's size by the hit's share of the target's max HP (chip / hit / heavy / whole swing). B keeps one size and sets weight, outline and opacity by share. Both keep the colour-by-kind and motion-by-kind rules and the lane stack. Left: a T1 Striker on a Moss Rat. Right: a Slinger in a swarm.

Reviewer's pick: A, size by share.

Size reads before the digits do, in the corner of the eye, which is the whole point of "numbers say share". B's weight ladder only separates its top and bottom rungs: at 15 px a 600 and an 800 weight look the same under a 3 px outline, and at the phone floor (right-hand frames below, the camera at 0.4) every rung of B collapses into one bold blur, while A's 25 px whole swing still stands out from a 12 px chip. A also gives the crit a free, honest look: a crit moves up one band, so on a rat it is the biggest thing on screen and on a boss it is a visible step above the chip-sized swings around it, without inventing a fifth size. A costs more vertical room, which the lane stack pays for: five live floaters a target, oldest dropped, never overlapping. B's one real win, steadier geometry in a swarm, is not worth a language you cannot read on a phone.

1 · A T1 fight on a rat

Striker (Axe, its Bleed) on a Moss Rat. Hits land at 9–10 % of the rat (band hit); the cadence finisher is empowered, 19–21 %, promoted one band to whole swing, gold, with a 110 ms body flash. Bleed ticks every 0.5 s and is coalesced to one number a second. The rat's 17s land on a 220 pool at 8 % (hit, damage-taken red); one is a graze (~). The overhead bar's chip holds the last hit for 0.4 s, then drains. Target frame: portrait slot (the atlas frame), name and tier, a proper bar with the number and the chip; homes and tiles unchanged.

2 · A Behemoth fight

Squire on the Crag Behemoth at 53 %. The boss frame sits top centre: name, (phase tile slot — the T1 Behemoth announces no named phase; the T2 example strip below shows one), a wide bar with quarter markers: the quarters already LOST are lit gold and counted in the pips, the next boundary is the white tick, so the third lit pip says the fourth firing is next. A slim cast line fills with the Crag Charge's 2.4 s (the client's castState from monster-cast-start). The 95 lands as 65 taken + 30 absorbed by the ward (whole swing against the pool). Swings of 21 are 1 % of the boss: chip-sized, honestly. The target frame drops its own name and bar under a boss frame for the same body (one channel per fact) and keeps the homes and tiles. Low health at a quarter: a steady red edge glow on the vitals.

3 · A Slinger in a swarm

Slinger (reload, light) against four Young Wolves, two Field Hares and a Grass Kite. Small bodies take a 24 px bar, the medium ones 32 px. A volley of eight shots on one wolf inside a second shows the cap: five live floaters per target, the oldest dropped first, never stacked on top of each other. Poison ticks coalesce per target per second.

4 · Overhead bars, the target frame, the vitals

Overhead bars by size class (desktop, zoom 1)

Width by body size class: small 24, medium 32, large 44, boss 72; height 4 (boss 5); minion/summon 24×2. Colour by thirds kept (green above 50 %, amber above 25 %, red), tokenised. The chip (pale) lags the fill. Absorb: a player's barrier+wards ride the fill as one cyan band; an enemy shell is the cyan band above the bar. Elites and bosses get a thin segment every 25 %. Bars draw no text. On a phone at the 0.4 floor the thickness keeps 3 screen px and the width stays body-relative.

The vitals (one bar module, three uses)

The vitals bar is the same module as the target and boss bars: chip, absorb band, number inside. Low-health treatment at ≤ 25 %: a steady, tokenised red edge glow on the bar's frame (no flash, no pulse). The ring beside it does not move.
