# Design Brief D3 — Tiers 5 and 6

**For:** an external design session. Self-contained — assume no prior knowledge
of this project.
**Returns to:** `docs/next-playtest-implementation-plan.md` Phase 3.
**Written:** 2026-08-02.

---

## 1. The game, in one page

**MMO Idle** is an automatic-combat idle RPG. The player does not control the
character in combat — they build it. All the decisions are *preparation*
decisions: class path, gear, cores, abilities, stances, rites, runes (a
programmable behavior language), party, and where in the world to go. The server
simulates everything authoritatively; the client renders and sends intent.

The stated player fantasy is **"you are the trainer/programmer of an autonomous
hero"** — a coach, not a fighter.

**The world** is a node graph, authored as regions. Each region is a tier. A node
is one screen-sized combat arena with monsters that respawn; you walk between
nodes through gates. Some nodes are dungeons, which contain an altar that starts
a curated encounter ending in a boss.

**Progression has two axes:**
- **Player tier** (T1…) — the big gate. Advancing unlocks a new region, deeper
  skill-tree tiers, more build slots, and higher-tier gear.
- **Biome level** — per-biome mastery, earned by fighting in that biome. Gates
  recipes. `BIOME_LEVELS_PER_TIER = 6`, so a biome offers 6 levels of content per
  tier it spans. **Global Mastery** = the sum of all biome levels; it drives the
  rune-point budget and the item-upgrade ceiling.

**Currency:** essence (5 colors, thematically named Might/Wild/Rot/Stone/Deep)
drops from kills; **catalysts** are a second currency keyed to a node's combat
"pace family." Both pay for recipes and upgrades. There is deliberately **no
trade and no player economy** — everything you have, you earned.

**Tier scope:** T1–T4 exist and are content-complete. The game is designed to
reach T8 eventually. **This brief covers T5 and T6 only** — they are permanent
game content (not playtest filler), and they must leave room for T7–T8.

---

## 2. What you are being asked to decide

The identity, roster, and content shape of **T5** and **T6**.

This is the single largest cost item in the project's roadmap. The decisions here
determine several months of authoring work, so precision is worth more than
ambition.

## 3. Grounding — the facts you must design against

### 3.1 The biome roster rolls each tier

Biomes enter and retire as tiers advance (~2 in, ~2 out). Current state:

| Tier | Biomes present | Entering | Retiring |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | mountain, cave, forest, plains, swamp | — | — |
| T2 | forest, plains, mountain, cave, swamp, jungle, desert | jungle, desert | — |
| T3 | tundra, mountain, cave, jungle, desert, volcanic, swamp | tundra, volcanic | forest, plains |
| T4 | mountain, tundra, jungle, desert, volcanic, graveyard, trench | graveyard, trench | cave, swamp |

Seven biomes per region from T2 onward. A retired biome keeps existing — it just
has no nodes at the higher tiers, so players stop going there.

### 3.2 Every biome has a locked mechanical identity

A biome is **a combat pressure shape plus an itemization home**. These are
canon and inherited upward — do not redefine them:

| Biome | Density | Combat pressure | Weapon home | Armor home | Charm home |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Plains** | high (24) | weak swarm; volume is the threat | Broadsword (no mechanic, universal floor) | plating | kill-burst heal |
| **Forest** | high (18) | fast, frequent, low per-hit | fast / low-per-hit (best proc-stacking) | evasion | raw regen |
| **Mountain** | low (12) | rare huge hits that trip the damage cap | slow / high per-hit (plating-piercing) | damage cap | periodic %shield |
| **Swamp** | med (10) | trivial direct damage, heavy stacking DoT | DoT-conversion | dot-resist | absorb |
| **Cave** | lowest (8) | few tough MIXED-shape elites | Chaotic Axe (every 3rd swing deals no weapon damage but still procs) | premium %DR | regen-burst pulse |
| **Jungle** | high (15) | aggressive, on-hit / hardening | on-hit rapier variant | hardening + evasion | ramps with combat duration |
| **Desert** | low (8) | "the standoff" — few tough, debuff-laden | Ambush (empowered vs full-HP target) | last-stand cheat-death + cleanse | cleanse / burst-heal-on-kill |
| **Tundra** | low-mid (8) | slow hard hitters; ice armor that shatters | frost / slow-debuff | hit-to-dot debt + bulk | bulk regen |
| **Volcanic** | high (18) | sustained heat/fire attrition | fire/DoT or burn-aura | bulk + regen | in-combat regen |
| **Wasteland** (`graveyard`) | extreme-high (20) | overwhelming weak undead; necromancers raise more | Plague (DoT spreads on kill) | "overwhelm" (mitigation scales with enemy count) | — |
| **Trench** | extreme-low (5) | rare abyssal terrors; anti-heal pressure | Execute (empowered vs low-HP) | heavy single-target damage cap | — |

**Density is the primary axis.** Notice the roster deliberately spans extremes at
T4 (Wasteland 20 vs. Trench 5). Any new biome must claim a defensible position on
this axis, not duplicate one.

### 3.3 Combat mechanics that already exist (recombine these)

New biomes should be built from this vocabulary rather than inventing more. The
project's stated principle is **complexity peaks at T4** — T5+ is recombination,
not new vocabulary.

**Enemy AI / ecology primitives:** packs (shared aggro, alpha buffs followers,
call-allies), patrol routes, swarm convergence, elite tags (yellow outline,
targetable by rune), `openingStrike` (pounce / alpha-strike), marks
(`appliesMark` + `markedStrike`, cleansable), ambient node-wide soft timers
(Volcanic heat), enemy shields with a shatter bonus (Tundra ice armor → break
grants bonus damage + freeze), `appliesAntiheal` (Trench), spawn-adds with an
`maxAlive` cap, ranged/kite/charge profiles, telegraphed charged attacks with a
cast bar, auras.

**Terrain:** node features that block, damage, or slow; hazard pools that can be
temporary or permanent; chokepoints; dense-bush ambush cover; lava vents.

**Node modifiers:** every non-boss node carries a "pace family" that reshapes
monster offense budget-neutrally, and re-keys which catalyst it drops.

**Dungeon structure:** an altar starts the encounter. Pre-encounter guardians can
be cleared first; survivors make the boss harder via one authored hook (`join` /
`empower` / `extra-adds` / `hazard`). T1 dungeons are pre-encounter + boss with
no waves; T2+ run multi-phase gauntlets. Bosses support phase scripts, enrage,
summons, regen windows, and telegraphed tells that the rune system can read.

### 3.4 Player-side systems that could grow at T5/T6

Everything here exists and works. The question is what T5/T6 *add* to each — and
what deliberately gains nothing.

- **Skill tree** — root → variant → range (close/mid/far) → T3 path. Six class
  archetypes: **Cadence** (every Nth hit is empowered ×2), **Cooldown** (a timed
  execution window arms every ~7 s), **Energy** (build a resource per hit, spend
  at max), **Reload** (ammo magazine + reload cycle), **DoT** (convert % of attack
  into stacking damage-over-time), **Summoner** (commanded minions). T4 specs
  exist; T5+ tree content is undesigned.
- **Gear** — 5 slots: weapon, armor, recovery (charm), mobility (boots), core.
  Items upgrade +1…+5 and can **evolve** into branching successor recipes.
- **Cores** — percentage multipliers on overall stats that reinforce your *role*;
  gated on matching your chosen range.
- **Abilities** — Technique (consumed by the next qualifying hit) and Guard
  (a duration buff). 2 Technique slots at T3, 2 Guard at T4.
- **Stances** — a default + a reactive stance, switchable by rune.
- **Rites** — always-on out-of-combat passives, 2 slots, T3+.
- **Runes** — a programmable condition→action rule language with a point budget.
  This is the game's signature system.
- **Relics** — *being designed in parallel (brief D2)*. They operate at the
  **mechanic** level and reinforce your class mechanic. Arriving at T4.
- **Seals** — tier advancement currency, minted by bosses. Replaces the old
  "kill a boss to tier up" quest.

### 3.5 The engineering constraints (these are hard)

- **A region is cheap.** It is authored as a ~45-line data file: a character-grid
  mask of which cells are nodes, a biome list, dungeon cell coordinates, and a
  sanctuary (respawn point). Adding T5 and T6 regions is trivial work.
- **Content is the whole cost.** Every biome present at tier T **must** have a
  monster pool for tier T or the server refuses to boot. Same for dungeon bosses.
- **A new biome costs art:** a ground tileset, a prop kit, and a full monster set.
  A *returning* biome at a new tier costs only monsters. **New biomes at T5/T6
  are wanted and considered affordable** — the art pipeline's measured throughput
  supports it. So this is a question of *which*, not *whether*.
- **The XP curve extrapolates automatically** (`25 × n^2.8`, offset by a biome's
  start tier). The real decision is wall-clock targets per tier, not curve shape.
- **Item upgrade ceilings are already tier-banded** and derive from biome start
  tiers, so they extend to T5/T6 with no new math.

### 3.6 The existing proposal (accept, amend, or reject)

An earlier internal doc proposed:

- **T5 = the AoE / pack axis.** Mobs spawn and act as authored pack compositions
  with shared aggro and an optional alpha that buffs the pack while alive. Player
  side gets an AoE-vs-single-target branch choice, governed by an **AoE budget
  rule**: AoE pays a per-target discount such that there is an explicit
  break-even density (~3 targets) — below it single-target wins, above it AoE
  wins.
- **T6 = layered defenses + twin bosses.** Enemies combine two defenses (shield +
  soft-cap; evasion + plating); mixed-shape packs. Boss layer: two bosses in one
  encounter with complementary shapes and a link rule (shared HP, or a mutual DR
  buff while both live).

⚠️ **Important caveat:** the pack primitive **already shipped at T1** (Forest is
built on packs with an alpha). So "T5 = packs" is weaker than it looks — it may
already be spent. Consider whether T5 needs a different lesson, or whether the
*player-side* AoE branch alone is enough to carry the tier.

## 4. Constraints you must not violate

1. **Complexity peaks at T4.** T5/T6 recombine existing vocabulary; they do not
   add new player-facing systems wholesale.
2. **Determinism.** No random mutators, no weekly rotating modifiers. The game's
   identity is that outcomes are decided by preparation, and the player can reason
   about them.
3. **No trade, no player economy.**
4. **No defense/recovery that scales with offensive output.** No lifesteal-shaped
   mechanics of any kind. This is a hard axiom, not a preference.
5. **Solo-completable.** Party play is incentivized, never required.
6. **Leave headroom for T7 and T8.** T8 is planned as the "void" callback tier
   with the Void Overlord finale. Do not spend the otherworldly/void flavor
   budget at T5–T6 — the flavor ramp should be: T4 mundane → T5–T7 progressively
   stranger → T8 openly otherworldly.
7. **Budget separation.** Offense, defense, and utility are separate budgets;
   items don't cross them.

## 5. Questions to answer

**Q1 — Tier lessons.** In one sentence each: what does T5 teach the player, and
what does T6? Given that packs already shipped at T1, is the proposed T5 = AoE
axis still right, or is there a better lesson available from the existing
vocabulary? What is the *trash* identity and what is the *boss-layer* identity
for each tier?

**Q2 — Roster.** For T5 and for T6: which biomes carry forward, which retire, and
which are new? How many biomes per region (7 is the current standard; 5 is an
acceptable smaller answer)?

**Q3 — New biome identities.** For each new biome, fill the same row shape as
§3.2: density value, combat pressure in one sentence, weapon home, armor home,
charm home. New biomes must claim an unoccupied position on the density axis and
an itemization home that isn't already taken.

**Q4 — The AoE budget rule.** If adopted: what is the break-even density number,
and does the discount live in a shared formula, an item stat, or both? If
rejected: what replaces it as the player-side answer to the T5 lesson?

**Q5 — Pacing.** Wall-clock target per tier. For reference, T2 is currently
~15–20 minutes of play. What should T5 be? T6? (Hours? Days? A week?) This number
drives all XP and essence tuning downstream, so an explicit answer matters more
than a precise one.

**Q6 — Build-system growth.** For each player system in §3.4: does it gain
something at T5, at T6, or deliberately nothing? At least half should gain
nothing — that's what "complexity peaks at T4" means in practice.

**Q7 — The T7/T8 hook.** What must T5/T6 explicitly *not* spend, so the last two
tiers still have somewhere to go?

## 6. Return format

Please return exactly these four artifacts. Prose beyond them is welcome but
these are what get implemented.

**A. Two tier briefs**, one page each, in this shape:

```
## T5 — <name of the lesson>
Trash identity:      <one sentence>
Boss-layer identity: <one sentence>
Why this follows T4: <two sentences>
What the player learns to do differently: <two sentences>
```

**B. Roster lists**, as bare biome-id arrays ready to paste into a region file:

```
T5: [<biome ids>]     new: [...]  retiring: [...]
T6: [<biome ids>]     new: [...]  retiring: [...]
```

**C. New-biome identity table** — one row per new biome, columns exactly:
`biome id | display name | density | combat pressure | weapon home | armor home |
charm home | 3–5 monster shapes | boss concept`.

**D. A decisions list** — one line each for Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7.

## 7. Notes on how this will be used

The roster (B) is wired into a data file the same day it arrives. The identity
table (C) becomes the monster-authoring worklist *and* the art-generation brief —
so the "monster shapes" column should be concrete enough to describe a sprite
("armored burrowing crab that surfaces to slam"), not abstract ("a tank").

Anything you leave open will be decided by whoever implements it, probably worse.
If you are uncertain between two options, pick one and note the alternative in a
single line rather than deferring.
